**“The Prophet of Essaouira”**

Pronounced S-Oh-Air\-Uh

 – 1 –

Jesus, what time is it?

A few minutes past noon.  That was the midday bell that woke you.

Noon!  Already!

In fact, we crossed the first time zone this morning, so it’s 1:00 back in the Canaries.

Jesus.

How do you feel?

Not good!  Miserable!  No, fifteen times worse!

Have some coffee.

No, thanks.  I’m not even close to ready for that.

I understand.

How long have you been, you know, like sitting there?

All night.

All night!  Jesus.  Just watching me?

Keeping an eye on you.  I also did some reading.  Listen, this is from the latest issue of “Time.”

“According to this theory, Markham and Kennedy walked to the Senator’s room in the Shiretown Inn, a block from the waterfront, while Gargan returned the boat to Chappaquidick and drove back to the cottage.  If this version is true, the question remains why Kennedy would conceal the facts and invent the swimming story.”

I have no freaking idea what you’re talking about.

I realize you’ve been away for some time, but haven’t you read the papers?

I guess I did hear something about Chappa … however you pronounce it.  And I heard we landed on the moon like a month ago.  But that’s pretty much it.  Except for Woodstock, if that counts as news.

There isn’t much more you need to know, I’ll grant you that.

Jesus, it’s even worse when I sit up.

You had quite a lot to drink.

Pass me my uh, you know, my clothes, will you.

I’ll avert my eyes.

Ha.  You seem pretty comfortable with this, uh, this situation.

It isn’t the first time something like this has happened in the history of the civilized world.

Or in the history of you?

Not lately.

Jesus, is this me swaying, or the boat?

It’s more you than the ship I’m afraid.  The ocean is calm this morning, and the ship is rather large.

Yeah, I noticed that back in Tenerife.  I thought the boat looked like the Prudential Building, tipped over.  Excuse me a minute, will you.

Certainly.

#   #   #

I’ll try a little of that coffee now.

Sugar?

Sure.

It’s still warm, but not hot.

I’ll never drink that much again.  Ha!  You know, I seem to recall saying that on one or two other mornings.  But, look, I’m afraid I really don’t remember very much about last night.

I’m not surprised.  How much do you remember?

The bar.  Flirting with you.  Drinking too much.  Saying too much, I guess.  Oh, and splitting the moon!  That happened, didn’t it?

You said it did, but honestly, I didn’t understand what you meant.

It’s supposedly one of the miracles of Muhammed – that he split a full moon into two halves – and when we were looking at the moon last night, at its reflection in the water, a swell came along, and for a moment it looked like it was split into two, though I guess it would have been a pair of full moons and not two halves.

I see.  Very poetic.

Yeah, I thought it was.  But did I really threaten to jump overboard and swim out into the reflection?

I’m afraid you did, yes.

Jesus!

I didn’t think you were entirely serious.

I don’t think I was.  Anyway, I remember a little about what happened when we got back here, but, I’m a little embarrassed to say this, but I don’t remember if, you know…

No, we did not.

Did I make like a huge fool of myself?

You might feel that way, but I don’t.

That sounds pretty bad.  Jesus, I’m sorry.

There’s no need to be embarrassed.  The scene was more or less a cliché.  I played my role, and you played your role, and I don’t think it was the first time you’d played that sort of game with a woman like me.  With an older woman.  Nor was it the first time that I’ve been with someone and something has gone wrong, although I have to say that it does not usually happen quite that way.

How do you mean?

I mean that it wasn’t that you couldn’t do what we were going to do because you were too drunk.  It was that you wouldn’t.  For an entirely acceptable reason, though.  Do you not remember that part?

I’m afraid it’s kind of coming back to me.

Things were going as expected and had proceeded to a certain point.  Then, rather abruptly, I thought, you pulled back.  “No,” you said.  Very emphatically.  You couldn’t let me … do that.  It wouldn’t be right.  Because of her.

I’m afraid I do kind of remember that part.

I knew who you were referring to.  You’d told me, at the bar, that you’d been with a girl for some months, and even though she’d known that you were going back to school in the fall, the breakup was traumatic.  Or, as you put it, “heavy.”  For both of you, but especially for her.  Is that right?

I guess.

What surprised me was that, while break-ups are painful, they do not always get in the way of what we were about to do.  To the contrary, they sometimes add a certain spice.  But it all made much more sense once you told me what had happened in … what was the name of that town?

Essaouira.

Yes, that’s it.  A very pretty name, for a sad place.

How much did I tell you about what was so … sad.

Not very much.

How much not very much?

Are you sure you want to do this right now?

No.  But I want to at least know what I told you.

In that case, the essence is this.  You told me that you did not simply break up with this girl.  She lost her life, and in a rather dramatic fashion.

“She” was Lu.

What?

Her name was Lu.  Didn’t I tell you that last night?

No, I don’t believe you did.

If we’re going to talk about her I think we should use her name.

I agree.  Yes, of course.  But do you … do you want me to continue?

I want to know how much I told you.

Well, off the coast of this town there’s a rock that sits just above the water on most tides, but not the highest tides.  You said that she, Lu, went out there one night, to put her life in the hands of Fate.  If it had been a full moon perhaps she wouldn’t have gone, but it was a new moon, which doesn’t rise until the morning, so she wouldn’t have seen it.  However, a new moon also brings high tides, and that was what happened.  Her body was never found, but she was almost certainly swept away by the high tide, and you feel tremendously guilty because of how it happened.

Jesus!  That’s a helluva lot more than “not very much,” don’t you think.

Of course, it is.  I only meant that you didn’t tell me very much about Lu herself, or your relationship with her, or why she went out to this rock.  So, while sad, and tragic, and understandably very traumatic for you, I don’t have a true feeling for the whole story.  That’s all that I meant, but I can certainly understand how what we were about to do would have been disrespectful of her memory.

I shouldn’t have told you as much as I did.

Perhaps not.  But it’s alright.

I’m still embarrassed.

Of course.  But let’s put it behind us.

#   #   #

I could send for fresh coffee.

No, thanks, I should be going.

Please have a little something to eat.  Just something light.

No.  Bad idea, food.

I understand, and, well …

Well what?

Nothing.

You looked like you were about to say something, and then you stopped.

You’re right.  I did have a thought.

Well, go on, then.  When someone starts to say something, I need to know what it was.  Was it something about the food?

No, something else entirely.

Which is?

I was just wondering, I’m not sure where this thought came from, but when we began talking, at the bar … did you expect to come back here with me?

No, I don’t think so.  I think I just intended that we’d get a little drunk, you know, like people do in bars.  I think I also intended that I’d let you try to pick me up, since you seemed to have an interest in that.  And I definitely intended to let you buy me a few drinks, since you seemed to have more money than I do.  Or don’t.  But no, I don’t think I intended to go through with anything.  Because of what happened.  On the other hand, it doesn’t totally surprise me that I did come back here.

I got a sense of that from you.

Which – that I didn’t intend to, or that I did?

Both.

Ha!  You’re probably right.  I probably drank as much as I did to break down my resistance.  I mean, it wasn’t that I didn’t find you attractive.  I found you sexy from the get-go, and in a way that’s very different from the girls I’ve dated, and as I got drunker I told myself that I could really use some sort of, you know, release, I guess, if you’ll pardon the expression.  You looked like you could handle yourself just fine, anyway.  So I thought it was a straight, “You can use me for a night if I can use you for a night” proposition.  I mean, was I way off or what?

No.  And I would add that there was a certain air of mystery to you that interested me.  Like the murder mysteries I’ve been reading, I meant to finish you in an evening.  In bed.  With the light on.

Ha!  But then …you’re right, it wasn’t a physical problem.  It was just that it would have been wrong.  Even blotto.  I mean, it’s only been – what? – a week.

This has been very difficult for you, hasn’t it.

Very.  Yes.

I wonder, during the first part of the evening, how much was it about the flirting, and how much you simply needed someone to talk to?

Some of both, I guess.  And I guess I knew that I might flirt too much, or talk too much.  Or drink too much!  Speaking of which, what’s that you’re drinking?

Bourbon.

Hair of the dog theory?

Not exactly.  Would  you like one?

Not before six.

A good rule.

#   #   #

Have you told anyone else what happened?

No one outside Essaouira.

The name sounds familiar.  I believe I was there once, many years ago.  You say it has a rocky shore, but the place I’m thinking of had a lovely beach.

It has that, too.  The rocks where it happened are up by the castle.

Yes, I remember a castle, too, now that you mention it.  And when did this happen?

Last Tuesday night.  Or Wednesday morning.

And in the week since then?

Well, I spent about half that time in Essaouira waiting for, you know, for things to resolve themselves, one way or another.  Her body was not found, but when I got what looked like a definite answer, I left.  I hitched up to Casablanca to try and change my plane ticket, which is when I lucked into this cruise idea.  Then I had to hurry down to Tenerife to meet your boat when it came down from Cadiz.

Why didn’t you fly home?

Well, for one thing, I wasn’t sure if I could hitch up to, you know, Luxembourg in time for my return flight.  Also, when the agent presented the ship idea I thought it would give me some time to think about what had happened.  With a plane it would have been, you know, you snap your fingers and, bang, you’re in New York.  Talk about a rude awakening!  I had the time, since classes don’t start till the 4th.  Also, I got a great deal on the ticket.  Some dude cancelled, I guess.

In fact, he died.

What!

Yes.  Up at Mallorca.

Jesus!  The dude died!  In my cabin?

No, in the main dining room.  But rest assured, it wasn’t the food, although I do hear that the food down there leaves something to be desired.  It was a heart attack.

If I’d know that ….  Oh, I don’t know, I guess I still would have come.  But maybe you’re right, maybe it would have been easier to just shoot back, put it out of my mind, and deal with it later.  I mean because it is … Jesus, whenever I stop and think about it I, uh, I … oh, fuck!  There I go again.

It’s OK.

I don’t know which is the hangover and which is, you know, what happened.

It’s mostly what happened, I’m sure.

Have you ever had someone close to you die?

Yes, I have.

But did it happen in a manner where you bore some degree of responsibility?

In fact, yes.

Well then you can maybe appreciate what I’m talking about.

Perhaps.  But I would also say that every situation is unique.

You got that right.

– 2 –

If I ask you a question to which you might take offense, would you be willing to listen with an open mind?

What kind of question?

Something about last night.

Maybe.  I mean, I guess I owe you one.

You don’t owe me anything, but here is the question.  Last night, when you said that you couldn’t – or wouldn’t – continue because of what had happened with Lu …

Yes?

Now that you’ve told me what happened, I can certainly understand that, as I said.  But is it possible that it was the other way around?

What?  That what was the other way around?

Is it possible that it wasn’t what happened to the girl, to Lu, that got in the way of having relations with me?  Is it possible, instead, that you used not being able to have relations with me as a way to bring up what had happened?

That sounds like kind of a complicated and backwards way to bring something up.

Complicated and backwards is sometimes the path things take when the direct route is blocked or somehow forbidden.

No.  I don’t think I approached you in order to talk about what happened to Lu.  I wanted to talk, yes.  But I didn’t intend to tell you about her death.  I’m sure of that.

That’s not what I’m saying.

I better take five or ten more aspirin because you’re losing me.

I believe you approached me for the reasons you described earlier.  But I wonder whether, at the same time, this other part of the story wanted to come out.  You kept it from me at the bar.  And I agree, I don’t think you came back here with the intent of talking about her death.  But then the opportunity presented itself, and instead of one form of “release,” as you said, you reached for another.

I guess it’s possible.

I don’t think we’re ever quite sure why we do some things, but I also think it’s possible.

But why would I want to tell you?  I mean, you’re so totally different from me, or her.

Perhaps for that very reason.

You mean like, “Somehow, because you despise me, you’re the only one I can trust?”

I wouldn’t go quite that far.

Ha.  You’re funny.  Hey, can I bum one of those off you?

I didn’t think you smoked.  You didn’t in the bar.

I quit, while we were in Essaouira.  I started again after, you know, what happened.  Then I quit again, yesterday, when I boarded the ship.  As a kind of penance.  And now, well, here I go again.

I’ve quit many times myself.

Then you know how, when you haven’t had one for a couple days, they usually taste god-awful.  But this one’s great.  What are they, anyway?  I can’t tell with the fancy case.

Dunhill.

I rolled my own in Morocco.

If I haven’t said this clearly let me say it now.  I’m very sorry for your loss.

Thank you.  Thank you very much.

#   #   #

Will you get therapy when you get back home?

I guess.

People don’t, always.  Especially young men.

What, are you like a therapist or something?

Hardly.

But you’ve seen a few, I’ll bet.

Some years back, yes, I saw a number of them.

D’you sleep with any of them?

Yes.

Ha!  You crack me up.  But yes, I probably will get some help.  It’s pretty expensive, I guess, but I could maybe do it with a counselor at school.  I assume from your question that you think I should see a therapist.

In general, I don’t care for them.  I have friends who’ve been dependent on therapists for far too long instead of taking responsibility for their own lives.  However, in a case like this, with a trauma, yes, I think you’d be well advised to see someone competent.

I take back what I said earlier.

Which was?

When I said we didn’t have anything in common.  I mean, it sounds like we feel pretty much the same way about therapists.

We may have other things in common.

Not too many, I don’t think.

Is it alright talking to me, Troy?

It’s OK.  Why?

In addition to seeing someone when you get back, I think it would be good for you to continue talking now, while things are fresh in your mind.  You’ve made a start, and that can be the most difficult part.

That would be quite the switcheroo, wouldn’t it.  You start out thinking I want to use your body, and it turns out that all I need is the one shoulder.  And why would you want to do something like that, anyway?

You interest me.  I find you intriguing.

Like one of those books?

No.  That’s just the usual trash one reads on a cruise.

Whereas, in my story, it’s real people who suffer?  And die?

A fair comment.

Did you know that in Shakespeare’s time they killed people on stage.  Literally.  People would let themselves be killed to free their families from debt.

I’m not sure that’s true, but I see your point.  There’s a certain amount of prurience in all of us, and there may be more in me than in most.

Not to sound too cynical, but are you also thinking that in the course of me telling you these things, and getting emotional, and sobbing on your chest, as I think I may have last night, it might just happen that … you know?

If that feels right, I wouldn’t be opposed to it.  I am not sure that it would be a good idea under the circumstances, but I rule nothing out.

You know, I like your directness, I really do.  But I don’t know about talking to you.  On the one hand, I think it would be totally inappropriate to talk to you about her.  On the other hand, I do find it hard to talk to myself.  Maybe because I know all my little tricks.  So I really don’t know, and on top of everything else my head is not in the greatest shape.

Understood.  Let’s set the question aside for the moment.  What would you say to some lunch?

Here?  In the, uh, spider’s nest?

No.  I could use some fresh air, and perhaps you could, as well.  I was thinking of the café by the adult-only pool.

Don’t you think that after the scene in the bar last night the adults, as you call them, might look at us like – I mean look at me  – like I was your trophy.

Even though you’re not.

People will assume I am.  I mean, just look at the timing.  They’ll assume that we did it five times last night and three more this morning, me being such a young stud and all.

Could you not stand their thoughts?

You know, if I really was your trophy, definitely not.  But since I’m still a free man, maybe.  Though I’d need to change and clean up.

I have a few things to do as well.  What would you say to 2:00?

OK to the free lunch.  But that will probably be the end of this … of whatever it is.

If so, it will be a more dignified ending.

Deal.  2:00.  By the pool.

Just ask for my table.

You have a reserved table?

I always eat at the same time.  So they hold it for me.

OK.  And what’s your … your full name?

Elza.  Elza Reyes.

Right, Reyes, with two syllables.  Sorry I didn’t remember that.

No need to be sorry.

And I’m Troy.  Troy McAllister.

Yes, I know.

– 3 –

I’m pleased you came.  I wasn’t sure you would.

I wasn’t either.  And you know, if I’d showed up by myself even one minute after 2:00, I’m about nine hundred percent sure that that skinny fellow in the white jacket would have pointed to the sign and told me I was five minutes too late.  You know, “Sorry, but lunch is only served until 2:00, sir.”  But they seem delighted to break the rules for you.

They hope it will result in a larger gratuity.

Look, I have to tell you something.  I actually got here a little before 2:00.  I came right after the mini-muster they made me do as a new passenger.  In case we hit an ice berg in the middle of the summer.  You weren’t here yet, but instead of sitting alone at your table like a, you know, a special delivery package, I waited for you to show up.  So I saw you walk over just now and, uh …

You hadn’t noticed before?

Actually, no, I hadn’t.

Not in the bar?

I don’t think you got up the whole time.

What about after you met me outside?  And we walked to my stateroom?

I noticed that you had a cane, and you limped, but I thought you’d just sprained your ankle playing shuffleboard or something.  Not that I was thinking really analytically last night.

What about this morning?

You hide it very well.

I make no particular effort to do so.

How bad is it?

The one leg is fine, but the other’s not much use.  One doctor suggested replacing it with a prosthetic.

Jesus.  What happened?

A car accident.

Were you the driver?

What makes you say that?

Well, it’s your left leg.  If you were hit on that side – I mean it could have happened that way, if you were the driver.

I was the driver, you’re right.  But we were not hit by another car.  I drove off the road.

Was anyone else hurt?

Yes.

The way you say that, did someone … die?

Yes.  My second husband.

Jesus.  Had you been drinking?

What would be your guess, Troy?

Sorry, an insensitive question.

But a fair one.  It was my fault.  I accept that, and I have had to live with it.

Did this happen like a long time ago?

Fifteen years.  No, it’s been more like twenty years now.

Jesus, I’m sorry.

About the accident, or my leg?

Both, I guess.  And I’m sorry I didn’t notice.

Would it have changed anything if you had noticed?

You mean in the bar?

Or later?

You don’t think I stopped because of that?

It wouldn’t be the first time.

No, it wasn’t that.  If I had noticed, it wouldn’t have changed anything.  I mean, I guess it would have been different, even a little weird, to be perfectly honest, but no, I don’t think it wouldn’t have, you know, prevented anything.  Or would it have?

No, the rest of me is OK.

Jesus, I didn’t mean it that way.

Does it change anything now, Troy?  I ask that question because it feels like it does.

In a small way, perhaps.

How so?

Not in a bad way.  If anything, I think it makes you more approachable.

Am I that intimidating otherwise?

Maybe not to your peers, but to me, yes.  I mean, you’re way older than I am.  And, I don’t know why, but I find women with dark hair scary.

Mine’s gone rather gray.

That doesn’t change the fact that you have dark hair.  The gray actually makes it stronger, somehow.  And none of the girls I’ve dated have worn much lipstick.  Maybe for some big event, but not in broad daylight.  And I’m guessing you’re rich and powerful, too.  Are you?

Which?

Are you very rich?  I mean that’s a good-sized diamond, and it looks real.

It is real.  But “very rich?”  No, I wouldn’t say that.  But “rather rich,” perhaps.

You live in New York, I think you said.

For the most part.

What do you do down there?

I purchase art and antiquities.  But not in New York.  Abroad.

Is that what you were doing in Spain?

Yes.

Is it your money?

My, you’re full of questions.

It’s a form of defense, I think.  When I’m nervous I talk a lot.

It was my father’s money, originally, though his fortune has diminished under my care.

Are you married now?

Yes.

But he’s back in New York?

At the moment I believe he is in the Caribbean.

What does he do?

He sails.

Is this number two?  Or are you up to like five or six by now?

In fact, he’s number three.

Ha!  Knew it!  And he’s not the jealous type?

Something like that.

#   #   #

Is that all you’re going to eat?

Yes, would you like any of it?

I’ve always hated to see good food wasted, and I hate it even more after the places I’ve just been.

Help yourself.

Just a couple fries, if you really don’t mind.  But here’s a question for you.  I mean, since I’m so full of questions.  I told you last night why I took the boat instead of flying.  But what’s your reason for taking the boat?  Aren’t you New Yorkers like always in a huge hurry?

My stated reason is that this is an excellent way to transport the artwork I’m bringing back, because when the provenance of a piece is “complicated,” the airport customs process can be awkward.  The other reasons are that that I’m in no hurry to return, and I can think of few better places to practice my favorite pastime.

Which is that?  Reading cheesy mysteries, or seducing innocent college students?

Neither.

No?  What then?

Drinking.

Ha!  You know, you really are a character.  You’re totally unlike any of the women I’ve ever dated.

It’s possible you haven’t dated any women yet.

There you go again.  But I know what you mean, and you may be right about that.

#   #   #

You’re feeling better?

A little.

Would it be my turn to ask a question now?

Sure.

You told me last night that you’d been reading Camus to work on your French.

Yeah.

You said you also read some Proust.

I tried, but the Proust was pretty tough.  I had to look up every other word, and even then I didn’t always get it because some of the sentences are like three pages long.

There was one line from Proust that you liked very much, though.  You mentioned it several times.

I know the one you mean.  “La réalité ne se forme que dans la mémoire.”  Reality only takes shape in memory.

You said – I’m not sure if you remember this or not – that that’s what you hoped to do on this voyage, to let the reality of what happened take shape.

Ahhh, so here it comes again.

You mean my prurient suggestion?

Yeah, I was kind of wondering when you’d circle back to that.

Should I stop.  Or may I continue?

It’s OK.  Go on.

What would you say is the central question you need to answer, Troy?

I don’t know.  Maybe it’s like that riddle where it’s not the answer that’s important, but the question.

Is the question whether she died or not?

No, I don’t think so.  I think it’s pretty clear that she died.

Then, is it the role you played?

I think that’s probably it, yes.

That’s what you said last night.

I did?

Yes.  And the way you said it concerned me.

What do you mean.

You said that what happened has caused you to question, deeply, who you’ve been until now, and you believe that this will lead to a major change in your life.

Yeah, I do believe that.  But is there something wrong with that?

I don’t remember the exact words you used, Troy, and it was also how you said it, but it sounded like a form of – forgive the expression – original sin.

What!

You said you feel extremely guilty about what happened, and you implied that you plan to use that guilt as the foundation for these changes in your life.

Is that a bad thing?  I mean to make a serious mistake, where someone dies, for Christ’s sake, and then try and learn from it?

It depends on whether the change is a true change inspired by the events, or merely a penance.  I think it also depends on the extent of your guilt, which is difficult for me to assess based on the small part of the story I know.  On the one hand what you said felt very deep and sincere …

But on the other hand it might have just been the alcohol talking?

Or the guilt, regardless of how deserved it might or might not be.

Look, I don’t know exactly how I put it last night, but let me say it this way, now.  Sober.  Or pretty sober.  I want Lu’s death to mean something.  I don’t think she died for me, you know, like you-know-who supposedly died for our sins, and you could maybe argue that I was less to blame than I feel like I am.  But I was there.  I could have stopped her.  And I didn’t.  I don’t think any court would convict me, and I don’t think she’d blame me for what I did, or didn’t do, but it would please her greatly to think that she’d helped me to move in this direction.

Was a lot of your relationship with her about moving in a new direction?

Sure, I mean, my whole trip was kind of about that.

And how would you describe this new direction?

Well, not to get overly philosophical, but I’ve always been kind of closed off and disconnected.  I tend to be suspicious, and a little cynical, and awfully critical.  Plus I can get very angry, at other people, and at myself.  But I’m beginning to see that those are partly just defenses, kind of like I said before.  You know, to push people away.  What Lu believed is that people need to not only be more loving, but to truly tell each other what they’re feeling, to open their hearts and let the world in, although in her case …

Yes?

I think sometimes she let too much in, and she kind of … I’m not trying to make a sick joke here, but she used this image a couple of times herself.  She said that sometimes she kind of drowned in her own empathy.  She took so much in – more than any one person could contain – that she’d feel herself going under.

Of Greek mythology?

Heck, yeah.  I would have tried almost anything at that point.

I was still sick, but I didn’t mind half as much.  And then I met her.

What did you think of that?

Well, on the one hand it sounded a little crazy.  On the other hand, my body was kind of purging.  Plus I liked the idea that I might be getting better.

And she was cute?

It kind of hides for a while, you know, and then, when it jumps back out, it just takes your legs out from under you.  I don’t know if I can really do this right now.  Or ever.
